# BearDen

A personal finance app for debt payoff, paycheck budgets, bills, savings, and connected bank accounts.

[Open BearDen](https://bearden.kurtismharrison.workers.dev)

**Bank connections:** Settings offers Plaid and Lunch Flow side by side. Plaid supports bank linking, account repair, refresh, and available credit-card/loan details. Lunch Flow keeps its existing connection and history. Banks disconnected during the previous provider removal need a new Plaid sign-in; revoked credentials are never restored. When Lunch Flow is connected, select any new Plaid cash accounts in Choose accounts before including them in totals. SimpleFIN remains retired.

**Accounts** opens Calendar, Bills, and Debt from three buttons at the top. Fixed car payments belong to Bills, with a separate amount and due date for each vehicle (including multiple payments to the same lender). Compact debt cards expand for statement details and payment history. **Add Account** offers a recurring bill (amount, category, due date, frequency) or a debt (starting balance, APR, per-paycheck minimum). The Income tab contains bank balances, income configuration, and the bill forecast; personal-spending charts live in Income. Both Income widgets have the peeking bear and a labeled refresh control. Budget configuration and Transactions are available only inside the Income tab’s income widget. Income configuration starts with First paycheck date, which anchors the every-two-weeks schedule. The Trackers cards have no Income or Accounts configuration buttons. Withholding uses private state and W-4 choices, including filing status and the two-job/spouse-works checkbox. Michigan rates fill automatically from the 2026 tables. Existing take-home amounts and saved net bonuses remain unchanged. New bonuses use the selected state, federal supplemental method, and payroll taxes; see [calculation scope and sources](docs/withholding.md).

**Add Account → Debt → Connect credit card with Plaid** uses Liabilities and Transactions. Existing bank connections can be reused in the same form. Connected debt cards show balances, credit utilization, APRs, statements, and posted payments. Schedule the issuer’s minimum and due date, supply clearly labeled estimates for missing fields, or match an existing tracker debt to avoid duplication. Checked paychecks update account payment status and estimated payoff balances; undoing a check or resetting checks reverses that status. Connected cards use an explicit tracker match and count checked payments within the current statement period. Payoff-plan balances remain separate from live issuer balances, which remain available in Details. Pending transactions and refunds are not treated as card payments.

The 30-day forecast offers **Full debt plan**, **Minimums only**, and **No debt payments**. Comparisons preserve regular bills (including both car payments) and do not change the saved payoff plan. Minimums use the plan’s saved per-paycheck amounts and other scheduled card minimums.

Bills, manual debts, and connected cards can link to a posted bank transaction from their editor. Matching uses the merchant and bank account, with an optional exact amount to separate payments to the same lender. Reference-number variations are normalized; unrelated merchants are not guessed. Available past payments appear immediately, and future syncs reuse the rule. Pending transactions, refunds, and repeated transaction IDs are excluded. Matching bill payments update derived paid status without creating another bill or changing paycheck checkoffs.

**Settings → Email accounts → Add Gmail account** connects up to five mailboxes through read-only OAuth. Each person signs in with their own verified Google account and gets a separate private budget, Gmail connections, Plaid connections, notes, and income settings. Connecting an extra Gmail mailbox adds it to the current BearDen profile; signing into BearDen with another Google account opens that account’s separate profile. The current BearDen login must remain active while attaching a mailbox. Existing Gmail connections migrate automatically, reconnects preserve links, and each mailbox can be disconnected separately. Refresh tokens stay encrypted on the server.

Saving a bill or debt searches the connected mailboxes for relevant emails. In its editor, **Email updates → Link an email** selects the sender, mailbox, and optional last four account digits to watch. Selecting an email rereads that exact message from its connected mailbox, rather than relying on the background cache. A readable bill amount fills immediately; an unreadable due date retains the current date with an explicit estimate label. When both are readable, both fields fill; **Save bill** applies them to Accounts, the calendar, and the forecast. These are one-time sender/mailbox and merchant/bank-account rules: future bills and payments are watched automatically. The selected statement takes precedence over a previous bank-based estimate and survives email-cache cleanup. Later complete statements update it automatically; incomplete reminders do not replace it. Common numeric and month-name dates are supported, with omitted years resolved from the email date. Only the statement's actual due date is labelled confirmed; future recurring dates remain estimates. Card statements fill missing or stale Plaid details and are labelled as email data. Ambiguous amounts/dates stay unchanged, with an explanation in the editor. Emails are read in bounded batches in background sync; manual searches return recent matching messages.

Credit cards show pending payments with expandable payment history. Regular bills, including car payments and non-card loans, use the matched bank transaction date and have no pending state or two-day timer. With Plaid, a submitted/scheduled payment stays pending until matching posted bank activity arrives. Without Plaid, an explicit completion email can confirm posting; otherwise it displays **Posted (estimated)** after 48 hours (starting on a future scheduled payment date when present). This is derived from the saved email timestamp, survives closing the app, and never rewrites starting balances or paycheck checkoffs. Failed/returned payment emails supersede earlier notices; a confirmed number prevents duplicate payment entries.

**Optional features:** Baby Bear mode is removed; income settings already persist in the private cloud profile. Settings has independent bonus-controls and shared-bills-account toggles. Hiding bonus controls preserves previously entered bonuses in every total. New profiles start with bonus controls off; existing profiles retain the previous enabled behavior unless they turn it off. Shared bill paying starts disabled. Preferences are private to each profile.

Background sync rotates between institutions, checks balances/transactions about every six hours, and fetches Liabilities about daily. Manual card refresh targets the selected institution. Tokens remain encrypted server-side; errors retain the last successful details. The Transactions button opens outgoing activity from the last 30 days; link editors can also search older available bank history. Personal spending is tracked separately and is not projected into the bill forecast.

Run `npm test` with Node 24 or newer. Tests cover card fields, payment classification, additional consent, private-data boundaries, forecast accounting, recurring-bill/debt separation, the income-only preset, Gmail account isolation, statement parsing, pending-payment transitions, merchant links, and forecast modes. Mobile browser checks cover account creation, Plaid cancellation/return, existing-data preservation, and keyboard navigation.

For browser color regression checks, run `npm ci`, `npx playwright install chromium`, and `npm run test:ui`. These checks render the shipped stylesheets and actual total-card markup at phone and desktop widths, in light/dark and holiday modes. They require at least 4.5:1 text contrast for totals, labels, filled/selected/disabled controls, warning messages, and bank summaries, and check overflow and keyboard focus. The GitHub workflow runs these checks on every push and pull request.

The finance palette follows Quill’s ivory/tide light theme and midnight/sea-glass dark theme, with serif page headings and compact paper cards. It lives in `public/theme.css`, loaded after component styles. Pair surfaces with their foregrounds: `--ink` on neutral cards, `--accent` on `--accent-soft`, `--on-accent` on `--accent-fill` controls, and `--on-dark`/`--on-dark-muted` on dark panels. Keep background colors in CSS rather than assigning unrelated inline theme colors.


**Private accounts and existing data:** `ALLOWED_EMAIL` now identifies the original profile whose saved data already exists; it no longer blocks other verified Google logins. Original records and sessions remain attached to that original profile. Other profiles use the stable Google subject, isolated encrypted records, separate sync locks, and distinct Plaid client identifiers. A stale browser tab cannot write to a different signed-in account. Scheduled sync rotates through profiles. No database schema migration is required.

**Shared bills:** Income → Budget → Fixed Bills lets the user choose distinct personal and shared Plaid accounts, their per-paycheck contribution, and their spouse’s contribution amount, first date, and weekly/biweekly/monthly schedule. Defaults in the editor are $725 each, applied only when saved. Select household bills in that editor or choose **Shared bills account — included in Fixed Bills** in a bill’s editor. The paycheck deducts one Fixed Bills contribution, showing shared bill payments and spouse contributions as informational details. The forecast switches between personal checking, shared bills, and both accounts. Personal transfers cancel in the combined view; spouse contributions enter the shared account only. Bank balances come from the selected accounts. Posted transfer pairs can confirm an own-account transfer; the same amount alone never identifies a spouse’s deposit. Projected contributions already reflected in a bank balance can be marked received and undone. Turning shared mode off restores individual bill reservations and hides spouse projections without deleting setup. Shared settings do not connect or merge either person’s private BearDen profile.

**Bills in paychecks:** Individual Accounts bills appear by name, amount, and due date on the paycheck covering that date through the day before the next payday. Unpaid bills from the two weeks before the first payday, plus overdue one-time bills, appear on the first check. Their amounts are reserved before debt allocations; the cash forecast deducts them once on their actual due dates. Card-funded bills, bills explicitly covered by another budget item, and linked tracker debt payments are not allocated twice. Already-paid bills stay in their original paycheck breakdown so payment recognition cannot inflate the payoff allocation. Income-only profiles also get paycheck cards. The old generic Fixed Bills starter item is migrated away; shared account contributions are configured separately so they cannot duplicate a budget item. New profiles and explicit resets start with editable Car Fuel ($100) and Groceries ($100).

**Google’s separate OAuth restriction:** BearDen cannot override Google’s testing/verification settings. In Google Cloud → Google Auth Platform → Audience, add the desired mailboxes to Test users while Testing, or publish the app for use beyond the tester list. Unverified external apps can still show warnings and have a user cap; public Gmail access is subject to Google’s verification requirements. Basic BearDen sign-in uses only identity scopes; Gmail read access is requested separately. See https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2/production-readiness/overview.

**Guided first-time setup:** New profiles start with Google or Microsoft sign-in, additional Gmail / Hotmail / Outlook inboxes, Plaid, paycheck date and income, budget, bill/debt creation, and a review. The actual app editors and connection controls work inside the walkthrough. Progress is private to the profile, persists through OAuth returns and reloads, and is marked complete only after a successful save. The two starter budget items can be edited or removed; no person-specific setup link is used. Existing configured budgets are not forced through onboarding. Settings can reopen it.

**Automatic cloud saving:** Signed-in data is saved to the encrypted server records and restored by the same Google or Microsoft identity on another device. The Settings status distinguishes server saving from signed-out device-only storage. Export controls have been removed; importing an older file remains available. Current-state saving is not a separate versioned backup archive.

**Forecast dates:** Income has 30-day, 90-day, six-month, and one-year shortcuts plus a custom end date. All forecast scopes and debt modes use that range, with running balances, the first negative date/amount, and the lowest balance/date. Long schedules display 100 entries at a time while totals include the whole period. The chart stores balance-change dates and preserves extrema when sampling a long range. Recurrences are not truncated at 2,400 events. If an unpaid debt extends beyond the payoff engine’s detailed schedule, the range note explicitly flags that later debt payments are not included.

## Bank accounts, balances, and transaction recovery

Plaid bank connections with loans now offer **Check balance connection** and **Enable / repair loan details** in Configure Plaid. The check reads the current Item's product access, connection error, and update time. The repair action requests Liabilities consent on the existing Item. When enabled and supported by the institution and app, BearDen reads closed-end/line-of-credit `principal_balance` and mortgage `current_principal_balance`, without confusing original principal or payment due with the remaining debt. A fresh positive Plaid liability balance can fill a missing or disputed general loan balance. Existing live positive balances keep priority. Neither the institution's loan coverage nor production product access is assumed.

SimpleFIN valid dated amounts are retained with an explicit stale warning when the provider also reports an account/connection error. Missing or invalid amounts/dates remain unavailable, and an error-associated zero cannot erase saved debt. Each account's Balance details shows the normalized amount/date actually returned, error codes, and whether its type/owed-amount setup is complete. No raw provider messages, access URLs, tokens, or full account numbers are exposed. Plaid troubleshooting identifiers are shown only inside the signed-in user's connection panel.

Settings → SimpleFIN accepts a one-time SimpleFIN Bridge setup token after the user signs in. Connect financial institutions in SimpleFIN, then create an Apps connection named BearDen and paste its setup token into BearDen. No new Cloudflare secrets or database migration are required. The access URL is encrypted in the existing per-user vault and never returned to the browser.

SimpleFIN imports balances and available posted transactions approximately daily. A lightweight, unfiltered balance request runs independently of the history request, so large or failed transaction responses do not prevent account discovery or balance updates. Manual refreshes have a five-minute cooldown and a 20-request daily cap that counts each provider request. SimpleFIN does not supply payment due dates/minimums; Plaid and email continue to provide their existing data. Assign each imported account a type; for debts, confirm whether the reported amount represents money owed or a credit. Edit an existing debt in Accounts to select its linked SimpleFIN account. Imported balances start hidden from cash summaries, so adding the same bank through two providers does not automatically double-count cash.

Missing or failed balances preserve the last known amount and are flagged unavailable. Structured SimpleFIN errors are scoped to the affected connection/account. A transaction-completeness error does not invalidate a valid balance; unscoped legacy warnings preserve valid reported balances and merge transactions without deleting prior history. Partial or failed history responses merge valid returned transactions without removing saved rows. The history response also recovers accounts or balances missing from the lightweight response. Status messages name affected accounts and translate provider errors into next steps without exposing raw provider messages. Saved cash selections and debt sign choices survive refreshes. Exact v1/v2 provider-ID aliases retain the original account identity, custom names, transaction IDs, and bill/debt links; ambiguous accounts from different bank logins remain separate. Existing disputed-zero protections remain in place: a reported zero does not automatically mark a debt paid off. Disconnect removes BearDen’s access URL and imported balances, while keeping manually entered debts and payment details. The user can additionally revoke the Apps connection in SimpleFIN.

Tests cover HTTPS host restrictions, redirect rejection, credential redaction, encryption, account isolation, sign handling, unavailable balances, reconnects, rate limits, and frontend token handling.

Plaid manual refresh checks both live balances and the full authorized account inventory, filling incomplete live values from cached bank data with a visible source label. Missing accounts retain their last known balances and links. Unconfirmed zero loan balances retain a previous positive principal when available; a conflicting positive cached principal is shown as needing verification. A first-time zero is labelled as unverified, not a confirmed payoff. **Settings → Configure Plaid → Choose / repair accounts** reuses the existing bank connection, requests account selection and transaction consent, and shows each bank’s balance and transaction status. Accounts still need to be shared by the provider; for SimpleFIN, manage BearDen’s permitted accounts in SimpleFIN’s Apps section.

Plaid history uses 500-row pages, up to five pages per Worker request, and continues remaining batches during manual refresh and after connecting. The committed cursor changes only after a complete update. An initial empty history response is labelled as still preparing and becomes eligible for retry after 15 minutes rather than six hours. Browser saves and provider actions are serialized. Server lock contention has a distinct retryable status and bounded client retries; revision conflicts still require reload. These operations retain per-profile storage and locks; no household data is merged.

### Sun Communities rent connection

On the deployed Cloudflare app, Settings → Rent → Connect Sun opens a five-minute Cloudflare Browser Run Live View link. The user signs in directly on Sun, selects their property, opens Payments, then returns to BearDen and selects Save connection. The Worker observes the portal’s own `GetCurrentBalanceBySelf` response. It stores minimum payment, current balance and due date separately. Settings → Rent offers a picker for an existing bill; the rent bill editor also has an Update amount from Sun portal checkbox. The selected Sun amount updates that bill, its calendar, paycheck allocations and cash forecast without creating a duplicate. Rent is scheduled monthly on the first; Sun’s account-level date is not used as the lease due date. Current balance is the default amount source, with minimum payment available explicitly. Earlier billing months retain their earlier amounts; later months use the latest amount as an estimate. A zero/credit balance retains the last positive estimate and does not mark a bill paid. No payment is submitted.

The Browser Run binding is `BROWSER`; no Cloudflare API token is placed in the frontend. Each connection uses an isolated browser. Recording is disabled and requests are restricted to the portal and supported sign-in dependencies. Only Sun’s sessionStorage `session` value and Sun cookies are persisted, in the existing user-scoped AES-GCM vault as `sun-session`. Password inputs, Google/Facebook sessions, live-view URLs and browser IDs are not included in public state or backups. The connect workflow still requires a valid BearDen session, matching origin and account identity. Treat the temporary Live View link as a credential. Disconnect removes the saved BearDen copy; Sun controls the original token’s lifetime.

The existing cron rotates through user accounts and checks each connected Sun portal about daily. A five-minute refresh cooldown avoids repeated browser launches. Expired logins or a different selected property pause automatic checks and ask the user to reconnect. Provider errors keep the last verified amounts. The local Node server shows this feature as unavailable; browser operations run only in the deployed Worker.

Cloudflare’s free Browser Run allocation is shared across the account (currently ten browser minutes/day). A login window consumes that time too. No paid plan is enabled by this change. See [Browser binding API](https://developers.cloudflare.com/browser-run/reference/browser-binding-api/), [Live View](https://developers.cloudflare.com/browser-run/features/live-view/) and [limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/browser-run/limits/). The Sun website adapter depends on the current portal response shape and session storage format, observed October 2026; this is not a published Sun integration API.

Personal spending and its forecast use only the selected personal checking account. The Accounts calendar is followed by Bills Account Spending and Bills Account Forecast, using only the bills account. The bills forecast has no debt comparison controls. Both forecasts offer 30 days, 90 days or six months, with no custom date picker or manual received-contribution buttons. Posted transfers can still be matched automatically.


**Microsoft / Hotmail / Outlook:** Direct Microsoft sign-in opens a separate private BearDen profile, without creating a Google account or reusing a Google profile with the same email. Optional read-only inbox access uses Microsoft Graph. Both Cloudflare and Node support this; follow [Microsoft setup](docs/microsoft.md) to register the app and configure its credentials. Gmail access remains separate.
